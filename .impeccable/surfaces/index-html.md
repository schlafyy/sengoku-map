## Scope

index.html: the whole app (one screen). Mode: Experience; the map leads and the interface recedes. Audience: the owner, at his Windows PC, exploring Japanese history. Opens at 1467, ready to play. Must not feel like a strategy game's campaign map or a textbook atlas; a little busy is fine, but not too much.

## Direction contract

THESIS: Japan as one length of indigo-dyed cotton. Each clan is a dye, and conquest is colour soaking across the cloth from the conqueror's side. This refuses both the gilded campaign map and the flat textbook political map with a legend box.

OWN-WORLD: The indigo ground is graded from kachi-iro (near-black) in the open sea to hanada at the coasts, with a faint cotton weave. Provinces are resist-dyed fields in natural dyes (bengara, akane, kariyasu, murasaki, yomogi, kaki-shibu, sumi, asagi), each clan a dye plus a tiny stencil motif. Pale resist lines mark the provinces. Undyed cotton means many small lords; kasuri stripes mean a split province. Sashiko running stitches are alliances, and a red stitched cross is a battle. Type is Mincho for names, kanji and the year, and a quiet gothic for UI.

STORY: The visitor sees the realm shatter after 1467, watches a few dyes spread until one cloth is nearly one colour by 1590, then splits east and west again in 1600. Clicking anything gives a short story first, with the full account, key people and alliances one step deeper.

FIRST VIEWPORT: At 1280×800, the archipelago runs diagonally across the left two-thirds on indigo. The year sits large in the empty Sea of Japan at top left, with its era name in kanji. A ranked clan roster (dye swatch, name, kanji, province count) runs down the right. A full-width woven band along the bottom holds the timeline, threaded with each year's clan shares. The play button sits at the band's left end, and threshold notches mark Ōnin, Okehazama, Honnō-ji and Sekigahara.

FORM: Indigo Cloth (katazome and aizome textile), position 5 of 7 on the ordered list. Raises: woven timeline band (VU Meter Bridge), dye ghost on change (Bioluminescent Wake), live clan roster with fallen clans undyed (Character Goods Catalog), thresholds that slow play (Accretion Disk), battle names set large across the sea for a moment (Raygun), and a palette law where every colour has one job (CRT Arcade).
